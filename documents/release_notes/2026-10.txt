Subject: Stewardship Atlas — Fall 2026 updates

Hi Stewardship Atlas fan and/or user!

It's been a busy few months. A lot of the work has been under the hood,
making the atlas faster, sturdier and ready to grow, which you won't see
directly. Meanwhile, here's what's new that you can see.


A SIMPLER, ROOMIER WEB MAP

We've cleared clutter off the web map so more of the screen is map:

  - The panel at the bottom left is now called Visibility. It controls
    everything you see, including the basemap. The basemap list now has a
    "None" option for when you just want your own layers on a blank
    background.
  - Sharing is now a single Share button. It opens a small window where you
    pick the kind of link (a link back to this map, a Google Maps link, or
    plain coordinates) and copy it. Ctrl-click (⌘-click on a Mac, or a long
    press on a phone) anywhere on the map to share a link with a pin on
    that exact spot.
  - "Go to…" opens a box where you can paste coordinates or a Google Maps
    link and jump straight there.
  - Both panels fold away with the small triangle, leaving just the map.
  - Every panel and pop-up has a small "?" that opens a short how-to for
    exactly that screen.


SHOW MY LOCATION

There's now a button that puts a blue dot on the map where you are and
follows you as you move. It's handy in the field for answering "where am I
relative to that hydrant / turnout / gully?" It's now on every atlas's
web map.


PRESET VIEWS

Atlases with named areas (runbook pages, zones, preserves) get a "Preset
Views" list on the web map. Pick one and the map jumps to that area with the
right layers showing. Each page of the PDF runbook also links to the
matching live view, so you can go from the printed page to the live map in
one tap.


BETTER-LOOKING RUNBOOK PDFS

Printed runbooks look more consistent: roads and creeks keep the same line
thickness whatever the page scale, map labels get a white outline so they
stay readable over busy backgrounds, and each region prints exactly as drawn.
You can also download the whole runbook as a single combined PDF.


EDITING: RESHAPE, AND SEE WHAT YOU'RE SELECTING

  - Reshape: you can now fix the shape of something that's already on the
    map (move a road bend, redraw a polygon edge) instead of deleting it and
    drawing it again.
  - When you draw a shape to update several features at once, the map now
    highlights exactly which features it will change and lists them before
    you save. No more surprises.
  - Photo pop-ups show the date each photo was taken. Photo labels only
    appear once you zoom in, so a busy map stays readable from far out.


MANAGE YOUR OWN LAYERS

Atlas administrators can now add, restyle and remove layers themselves from
the console:

  - Add Layer: upload a GeoJSON file, or start an empty layer to draw into,
    and pick its label, colour and line width.
  - Edit Layer: change an existing layer's colours, fill vs. outline, and
    whether it's showing when the map first opens.
  - Delete Layer: removes a layer cleanly. It won't let you delete one that
    something else depends on, and a deleted layer's data is kept in an
    archive in case you change your mind.


NEW ATLASES: UPLOAD A BOUNDARY, AND A "FIELD TRIP" STARTER KIT

At https://fireatlas.org/create you can now upload a GeoJSON boundary (a
parcel, a preserve, a watershed) instead of drawing a box. There's also a
new Field Trip starter kit for event and outing atlases. It comes with
roads and creeks tuned for zooming in and out, recent threatened-species
sightings from iNaturalist, and email-in photos so participants can add
pictures from the day.


NEW KINDS OF MAP DATA

These are new data we can now add to an atlas. Let us know if you'd like
any of them in yours:

  - Hexagon summaries: the area divided into a honeycomb, with each cell
    shaded by a count or measurement, such as building density,
    threatened-species sightings, or tree canopy height from lidar. It's an
    easy way to see patterns across a whole landscape.
  - Lidar canopy height and past burn severity (from the national MTBS fire
    records). These are useful side by side for seeing how past fires
    changed the forest.
  - Lidar-based hillshade basemaps, shared between neighbouring atlases so
    they line up seamlessly.
  - For the technically inclined: ready-made analysis notebooks that ship
    with your atlas, for digging into the data yourself.


CONNECTING OVERLAPPING ATLASES

Last time we said this was coming soon, and it's here: atlases that overlap
can now share layers with each other. Sharing is opt-in and owner-controlled
— you choose exactly which layers your atlas offers, and nothing else leaves
it. It isn't self-service yet, so if you think it would be useful — a
neighbouring department, a landowner who wants to share water access with
their fire department — get in touch and we'll set it up.


AN OLDIE BUT A GOODIE: EDIT IN GOOGLE SHEETS

This one has been around for a while but never made it into one of these
emails. Any layer can be exported to a Google Sheet, edited there — fix a
column of names, fill in missing details, share it with a colleague — and
imported straight back into the atlas. It's the fastest way to make lots of
changes at once. Find it under "Spreadsheets" on each layer in the console.


A NEW CONSOLE, AND BETTER HELP

The redesigned console we previewed in the summer is now the only one, on
every atlas — old links and bookmarks take you straight to it. The help
pages have been rewritten to match, with a short page for each task and two
guides tying them together: one for using an atlas, one for looking after
it.
