Subject: Stewardship Atlas — Summer 2026 updates

Hi Stewardship Atlas fan and/or user!

Here's a look at what's new in your atlas since the last update.


A CLEANER CONSOLE

The console you use to open maps and edit data has a new interface available — layer
buttons grouped by type, common actions in a tab bar along the bottom, and
a layout that works better on a phone.  To try the new
version, click the A/B button at the top right of the current one - you can 
do the same to switch back. 


MILEAGE MARKERS ALONG ROADS (AND MORE)

We can now add mileage markers to a road, creek, powerline, or anything 
where you might use distance  along a path. Let us know if there's a line
feature in your atlas you'd like this added to.


SHAREABLE 3D VIEW

The 3D terrain view now has its own share link. It remembers your exact
camera angle and which layers you have showing, so you can send someone a
link that opens to precisely the view you're looking at — the ridge, the
tilt, the layers, all of it. Good for briefings and for "look at this
drainage basin!" conversations.


INATURALIST OBSERVATIONS

We can now add a layer of community nature observations from iNaturalist
for your coverage area — plants, wildlife, and other sightings that people
have recorded and mapped. Useful background for vegetation and fuels work,
and interesting in its own right. Let us know if you'd like it turned on
for your atlas.


CREATE YOUR OWN ATLAS

You can now spin up a brand-new atlas yourself, no waiting on us. Go to
https://fireatlas.org/create, draw a box around the area you care about,
give it a name and pick a "starter kit" of the kind of data you're 
interested in, and submit. An atlas is built for that area with a
webmap, edit tools, and the usual layers, and you're dropped straight into
it. Give it a try on a neighborhood, a parcel, a favorite hiking area or conservation project.


COMING SOON: CONNECTING OVERLAPPING ATLASES

We're starting on a way for atlases that overlap to share
layers with each other. Sharing is fully opt-in and owner-controlled
— nothing leaves your atlas unless you choose it, and you pick exactly
which layers you offer. Worth thinking about now: does anyone near you map
the same area? With self-service atlases above, that "anyone" could be
you spinning up a second one. Landowners can track details only they
need to know about, but share things like water access or turnarounds with
their local fire department.


As always, reply to this email with any questions or if anything looks off.
